01 / About

From a research idea
to a working system.

My work sits between machine learning research and the engineering it takes to put that research into practice.

At LinkedIn, I've worked across Feed ranking, content exploration, off-policy evaluation, Learning Search, and misinformation detection. That has meant both building models and building the infrastructure to understand how well they work.

Before LinkedIn, I worked at IBM Research on learning to answer complex questions by generating executable programs. Earlier, at IIT Madras, I studied how reinforcement learning agents could share knowledge across text-based games.

The settings are different, but a recurring question connects them: what can a system learn from the feedback it has, and how can we tell whether it is making better decisions?

From M Passes to One ↗

A MapReduce-Style Bootstrap for Pointwise Off-Policy Evaluation in Production Recommenders

Ghulam Ahmed Ansari and Cheng Ding

Estimating a recommendation policy's performance from existing logs is only part of the problem. This work makes estimating the uncertainty around that result more practical, computing multiple bootstrap estimates in one pass over the data.

Read the paper ↗Off-policy evaluation · Scalable estimation
IJCAI2019SSRP

Learning when feedback is noisy

Neural Program Induction for KBQA Without Gold Programs or Query Annotations ↗

Ghulam Ahmed Ansari*, Amrita Saha*, Vishwajeet Kumar, Mohan Bhambhani, Karthik Sankaranarayanan, and Soumen Chakrabarti * Equal contribution

Learning to generate programs for question answering becomes harder when input annotations are noisy. SSRP uses continual retrospection, comparing current learning with a reference model, to reduce noise-induced instability.

Read the paper ↗Program induction · Sparse rewards
TACL2019CIPITR

Reasoning through executable programs

Complex Program Induction for Querying Knowledge Bases in the Absence of Gold Programs ↗

Amrita Saha, Ghulam Ahmed Ansari, Abhishek Laddha, Karthik Sankaranarayanan, and Soumen Chakrabarti

How can a model learn multi-step reasoning without examples of the correct programs? CIPITR combines neural program generation with symbolic constraints and auxiliary rewards to search for executable answers.

Read the paper ↗Weak supervision · Knowledge bases
Deep RL Workshop2016NeurIPS · arXiv 2018

Sharing knowledge across environments

Language Expansion In Text-Based Games ↗

Ghulam Ahmed Ansari, Sagar J P, Sarath Chandar, and Balaraman Ravindran

Can one agent learn from several games with different vocabularies? This work studies policy distillation from specialized agents into a shared agent, using text-based games to examine what transfers.

Read the preprint ↗Policy distillation · Language
